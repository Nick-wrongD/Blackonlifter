Plyometrics First

Always train explosive movements before heavy lifts — CNS must be fresh for max power output.

Triphasic Loading

Eccentric → isometric → concentric control for tendon health and elastic energy storage.

Intent Over Weight

Move every rep as fast as possible — bar speed drives explosive adaptation, not just load.

Unilateral Work

Single-leg and single-arm movements close strength gaps and mirror real sport mechanics.

Rest Between Power Sets

Full 2–3 min rest on explosive/strength movements — partial recovery kills power output quality.

Contrast Training

Heavy strength set followed immediately by explosive movement — Post-Activation Potentiation (PAP).

REST & RECOVERY

Wednesday · Active Recovery Optional

Sleep

8–9 hours minimum. The CNS needs recovery after explosive training. Growth happens here, not in the gym.

Hydration

3–4 litres of water. Add electrolytes if training intensity is high. Dehydration destroys performance quality.

Nutrition

Keep protein at 1.8–2.2g per kg bodyweight. Carbs refuel glycogen depleted by explosive training.

Active Recovery

Optional: 20–30 min walk, foam rolling, light stretching. Nothing that taxes the CNS.

🧬 Core Principles of Intermediate Training

01

Progressive Overload

Add 2.5–5 kg to your main lifts every 1–2 weeks. Or add 1 rep. Track everything obsessively — what gets measured gets improved.

02

Explosive Intent Always

Every rep of every power and plyometric movement should be at absolute maximum intent. Partial effort produces partial results. Full throttle or go home.

03

Respect the Rest Periods

Power sets need 2–3 min rest. This is non-negotiable. Cutting rest periods turns strength training into garbage cardio. CNS recovery drives adaptation.
